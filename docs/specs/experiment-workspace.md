## 1. Outcome and scope

A scientist can select an experimental question, assay and materials, design the conditions and plates, prepare liquid additions, review the relevant decisions and obtain supported worklists **inside one campaign/experiment workspace**. The assistant reads and changes the same scientific objects through the same operations. Opening a compound, kit, stock tube, instrument or protocol keeps the experiment and current selection visible.

The common navigation is **Design · Plates · Transfers** for compound screens, ELISAs and cell-based assays. These are places to work, not compulsory approval stages. Washing, incubation, reading and other protocol steps remain visible in the protocol summary; this work does not implement their scheduling or execution. Material production, including plasmid production, is a separate deferred journey. Analysis and hardware control are outside this delivery.

The product must support both **5,000 selected compounds** and **200 destination plates**, with additional source/intermediate containers. They are separate scale cases: 5,000 compounds do not necessarily require 200 plates. The mockup's larger synthetic subject count is not an approved expansion of the experiment-design limit.

## 2. The scientist's journey

| Moment | What the scientist sees and does | What persists / next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start | Search campaign and assay; state the question; choose an existing draft to resume | Campaign association and named experiment; incomplete inputs remain explicit. **Continue design** |
| Choose what to test | Search compounds, samples, sets or source plates; choose records or resolve the contents of selected plates; see included/excluded counts and early stock issues | An explicit material selection, its origin and exclusions. A source plate selection does not turn the plate itself into a compound. **Use selection** |
| Design | Set assay-specific targets, controls, replicates and readout; choose a kit in the ELISA example; review defaults and unresolved scientific choices | Values in the experiment's protocol inputs/bindings and design, with evidence. **Review design** / **View plates** |
| Plates | Browse destination plates, search a compound or well, view concentrations and control placement, adjust supported layout settings | Versioned map/overrides; local view settings do not alter science. **Review plate design** |
| Transfers | Choose a liquid addition; see sources, destinations, instrument, settings and worklist together; resolve missing stock or setup | Typed addition intent and generated transfer plans, with exact input references. **Calculate transfers** then **Review transfers** |
| Review | Summary of the exact design or transfer scope, changed values, assumptions and exceptions; direct fixes alongside the work | Existing people-only confirmations with fresh version/check validation. **Confirm design** or **Confirm transfer plan** |
| Worklists | Persistent output list on Transfers and a compact experiment summary, independent of chat; preview/download by step, device and plate batch | Durable association to exact plan/group/version and export result. **Generate worklists** / **Download** |

The first screen after drafting answers: what is being tested, which choices are still needed, what has been calculated, and which action moves this experiment forward. It does not lead with section counts, record lifecycle mechanics or a blanket claim that the protocol works.

## 3. Persistent workspace and navigation

Keep campaign, experiment title, scientific purpose and **Design · Plates · Transfers** visible. A compact summary reports the selected materials, conditions, plate count and unresolved requirements with their scope. Record codes and history are secondary details. Draft status is shown once; provenance appears next to the affected scientific value.

The workspace is a projection over existing records, not another authoritative experiment or a replacement workflow engine. Use the existing experiment route with explicit section/selection state; keep direct source-record links valid for registry work. Within the workspace, **View compound**, **View stock**, **View instrument checks** and **View protocol** open contextual detail. A deliberate **Open full record** action carries a return link preserving experiment, section, addition, plate, selection and filters.

Default detail presentation: a resizable side panel on wide screens, a full-width contextual sheet on narrow screens. Closing it restores focus and scroll. The assistant and detail panel must leave usable plate/worklist space, with a full-width view available. Back/forward and reload restore valid selections. An invalid/deleted/stale selection shows a reason and a recovery action rather than quietly selecting a different plate.

There are two kinds of state:

- **Scientific state:** saved inputs, bindings, material selection, plate assignments, transfer intent/plans and accepted versions. Operation-backed, shared, version-checked.
- **Viewing state:** tab, search/filter, color scale, visible columns and focused wells. Per person/client, representable as a typed view request. It never changes scientific meaning, confirms a record or reserves stock. Persist a compact route/preference where appropriate; large selections use a referenced selection rather than thousands of URL parameters.

## 4. Screen specifications

### 4.1 Campaign, assay and material selection

Use searchable browser panels with labeled results, relevant scientific facts, counts, keyboard navigation and paging. For collections with around ten or more choices, do not use a long dropdown. Small fixed enums such as unit or read mode may use compact choices.

Campaign results show purpose and recent experiments. Assay results show what is measured, compatible material types, protocol and what it requires. Material results adapt to the selected kind: compound identity/library/stock evidence, sample identity/specimen information, or source plate barcode/type/contents. Search supports identity/name, barcode and supported registry attributes; unsupported chemical structure/substructure search must not be implied.

Allow **Select this page**, **Select all matching results**, explicit exclusions, named sets and plate-content expansion. Show the final unique count, duplicate/missing identities, eligibility problems and the exact selection scope before applying it. Freeze all-matches membership at an identified selection snapshot; changing inventory or a search result later must not silently change a saved experiment. Keep physical stock assignments separate from selected scientific identities. An agent uses the same selection resolver rather than typing 5,000 IDs into prose.

Show stock information early as recorded/enough/short/unknown with a checked-at time. Lack of a lot or physical source may remain a preparation obligation when the scientific design permits it; do not block all design exploration on run-day stock. Physical facts are read again when needed.

### 4.2 Design: scientific inputs, not All fields

Use stable headings **Question**, **What is tested**, **Conditions and controls**, **Plate design**, **Readout**, and **Still needed**. Only applicable fields appear. Assay-specific subsections come from typed template/SOP roles and supported renderer definitions; no separate page tree per assay.

| Example | Scientific fields and labels | Important distinctions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compound screen | Compounds; concentration or concentration series; assay volume; solvent limit; vehicle and positive/negative controls; replicates; readout | Stock vs final concentration; actual solvent fraction vs ceiling; each control's material and target; no inferred positive-control dose |
| ELISA | Analyte/target; kit or defined reagent method; samples and matrix where declared; dilution; standard series; blanks/controls; replicate design; readout | Product vs kit lot/component lots; standard concentration units and reference; dilution relative to original specimen vs an additional dilution step |
| Cell-based assay | Cell model; conditions/treatments; target cells per well; treatment concentration; controls; incubation/readout choices from protocol | Cell identity vs physical suspension; density vs dispense volume; cell-containing vs no-cell controls; treatment and readout additions remain distinct |

**Required now**, **Needed before transfers**, and **Optional** are server-derived classifications tied to a named acceptance boundary. Requiredness comes from accepted science and capability contracts, not an agent's choice of labels. Optional means it can be omitted at that boundary; unknown critical facts cannot be relabeled optional. A required field has a direct Choose/Change/Edit action and a short explanation of why it matters. Optional details start collapsed. Non-applicable fields are omitted with an explanation available when useful.

Origins (**Template default**, **Agent proposed**, **Entered**, **Recorded**, **Calculated**, **Unknown**) and acceptance are separate. Confirmation of an estimate does not make it a measurement. Use existing field/item evidence and calculation handles.

**Kit selection:** Design → Analyte/kit → **Choose kit** opens a searchable registry picker. A compatible registered kit may supply a target only when that association is explicitly recorded. Multiplex or ambiguous kits require target selection; a custom reagent assay may have no kit. Show supported method/version, matrix/species information when recorded, component identities and unresolved specifications. Selecting a product does not invent its dilution, standard series, volume or compatibility. Lots and stock are chosen during preparation unless the design specifically depends on them. **Change kit** previews affected standards, bindings, calculations, maps and transfers before applying the change.

**Draft continuity:** before all essentials are answered, show a saved incomplete experiment, not a failed designer result. Reuse `experiments.draft` and experiment-owned inputs/subjects/bindings. The proposed `designer` completion operation fills that existing draft and creates/updates its derived maps without creating a second experiment. Preserve the existing `designer.start` complete-input contract. Before a campaign/question exists, the start panel may retain a clearly labeled local unsaved form; it must not display Saved.

### 4.3 Plates

Lead with useful totals: compounds/samples/conditions, technical replicate definition, destination count/format, control pattern, unused/excluded wells and unresolved placement issues. Counts and occupancy come from server calculators/projections.

- A searchable, paginated **plate navigator** supports barcode, number, sample/compound, batch and issue filters; previous/next, jump by number and a compact overview. It navigates all 200 destination plates without a 200-item menu or rendering every well at once.
- A plate viewer supports color by role, target concentration, planned volume, selected addition and issue state where those data exist. Each view has units, a continuous/categorical legend, explicit unknown/empty/excluded marks and an accessible alternative to color. A shared scale across compared plates is the default; changing to a per-plate scale is visible. Zero and unknown differ.
- Hover/focus tooltip: plate and well, material/condition/control, target with unit, replicate/series point and relevant issue. Selection opens persistent detail with planned additions, source relationship, stock vs target, evidence and links within the workspace. Do not use a tooltip as the only way to read essential information.
- Search highlights every match across the plate set and provides **Next match**. Selecting a well or material can highlight its replicates and all linked transfers without changing assignments.
- Supported edits include control/subject placement, exclusions and layout strategy; show a preview/diff, affected plate scope, conflicts and recalculated totals. Multi-plate changes require an explicit scope. Saving a reusable layout explains that material identities are not copied.
- Destination maps represent planned experimental contents. Source views represent inventory containers and recorded contents, with demand/reservations overlaid. A source tube is a tube with quantity/connection details, not a fabricated plate grid.
- Include **Stock · Intermediate dilutions · Assay plates** as searchable plate-role navigation where applicable. An intermediate is a destination during its preparation and a source during dosing; retain its identity between views. Show its planned composition and downstream demand without implying that it already contains prepared liquid.

### 4.4 Transfers: additions, maps, worklist and settings together

Use an addition list named for lab work: **Add compounds**, **Match vehicle**, **Add assay mixture**, **Dilute samples**, **Add standards**, **Seed cells**, **Add detection reagent**. Names come from the supported protocol's material roles/step identities. The assistant may propose clear labels but cannot silently add, omit or reorder scientific steps.

For the selected addition, show its purpose, material, required destination scope, progress/issues, selected device and next action. Keep **Map · Worklist · Settings** as views within Transfers; worklists and saved files remain directly reachable from this main screen.

| Area | Required content / interaction |
| --- | --- |
| Sources | Separate searchable navigator for source plates/tubes/reservoirs; material/lot, stock concentration, recorded/usable volume, reservations, demand, shortage/unknown, loading assignment. Multiple containers and replenishment batches are explicit |
| Destinations | Searchable plate navigator plus wells receiving this addition. Show all linked destinations/sources and allow choosing among them; never silently follow only the first counterpart |
| Maps | Side-by-side source/destination where applicable; material and transfer highlighting linked to the worklist. Tube cards for nonplate sources. A relationship overview summarizes many-to-many mappings without drawing thousands of lines |
| Worklist | Server-paged/virtualized rows: material, source container/well, destination plate/well, planned volume/unit, relevant concentration and exception. Filters share the map selection. Stable row identity survives sort/filter; aggregate totals cover the stated full scope, not only the current page |
| Settings | Inherited scientific targets, independent preparation inputs, device/liquid-class/format choices, calculated outcomes and exact unresolved facts. An inherited target has **Change in Design**, not a duplicate editable field |
| Checks | Specific results such as volume increment, source volume or incomplete control definition, with evidence scope and direct fix. No global green “the protocol works” from numeric evaluation alone |
| Outputs | Files grouped by addition/device/plate batch, version/freshness, included and skipped scope, Preview/Download/Regenerate. Files stay available after reload and outside chat |

Echo sources are plates where supported by the registered device/liquid class. Mantis and PreciseDrop II sources in this workflow are tubes. Their source labware, installed dispensing equipment and verified connection/port information must come from the registry and inventory. Current generic instrument sites and record links do not supply that missing fluid-path contract.

**Instrument detail** answers whether this particular addition can be prepared with it: registered identity, applicable capability, plate/source compatibility, volume range/increment, installed head/chip, liquid-class evidence, configuration/connection needs and relevant status/calibration facts. Missing serial number is supporting maintenance information; it must not crowd out a missing dispense configuration. Never display mock seeded formats as device-validated methods.

**Compound dose-response and intermediate dilutions (required):** when original stock is too concentrated to reach a requested curve point within the registered transfer limits and allowed error, calculate a supported intermediate dilution. Route each point directly or through an intermediate as the calculator permits; do not silently change the requested curve or drop unreachable points. This preparation stays within the experiment, including single-point dosing when it needs a dilution.

Show **Stock → Prepare intermediate dilutions → Dose assay plates**, retaining direct stock-to-assay routes where applicable. Within the same map/worklist/settings views, provide:

- A searchable curve-point table: compound, requested final concentration, achieved concentration/error, direct/intermediate source, transfer volume and droplet count when applicable, plus the reason a point cannot be reached. Summarize affected compounds/points before drilldown at scale.
- Intermediate plate/well details: originating stock, dilution factor and resulting concentration, stock and diluent volumes/sources, total preparation volume, required downstream volume and unusable residual volume. Distinguish source, intermediate and final assay concentrations with units.
- Preparation and dosing worklists, each with its actual selected device/settings and dependency. Include diluent addition and any method-required mixing/preparation instructions; do not assume the dosing instrument can perform every preparation step. Missing supported preparation remains an explicit unmet requirement.
- Actions **Calculate dilutions**, **View intermediate plates** and **Review preparation** through the same operations available to the agent. Changed stock concentration, curve, replicate count, assay volume or preparation settings recalculates affected routes and marks dependent outputs out of date; never silently relax tolerance or solvent limits.

### 4.5 Review and worklists

Review appears beside what will be accepted. Show scientific summary, changes since the last accepted version, blocking issues, explicit unknowns/assumptions, and the exact records/versions included under **What this confirms**. Review 5,000 compounds by coverage, distributions and exceptions, with drilldown; do not require opening every source record or approving every row.

Preserve existing people-only operations and proposal authority. **Confirm design** and **Confirm transfer plan** are distinct. Confirming a group view must not falsely imply group-only confirmation if the operation confirms the whole plan. Until smaller scopes are supported, display the whole plan scope. A multi-record action uses existing atomic confirmation/change-set rules only when eligible; otherwise explain the required scientific step within this workspace.

Disabled actions say why and offer a fix. While an assistant is working, show the affected draft and saved progress; do not hide all manual actions behind an indefinite busy message. Concurrent writes use expected versions and refreshable diffs, preserving unsaved user values. Navigating a view does not accept a pending proposal.

Export is separate from confirmation. Each export shows coverage: generated, skipped, blocked, and why. Old files remain historical; a changed material selection, map, addition or setup marks affected outputs **Out of date**. A file's bytes being deduplicated does not mean its association to the current plan/version can be recovered from the file's first origin. Persist an export receipt associating file, plan version, group, format, relevant input/configuration snapshot and simulator result where applicable.

## 6. Proposed model changes and contracts

These are bounded extensions of existing modules. Names below are proposed contracts, not callable operations today. Schemas and operation definitions precede API/UI work. Prefer an existing field when it has the right scientific meaning; do not duplicate a value merely to fit a screen.

### C1. Workspace reads and material selection

Add an experiment-owned read operation (`experiments.workspace`) accepting the experiment/version, supported section, optional addition/map/plate selection, filters and bounded page requests. Return a header, referenced versions, counts with scope, relevant scientific requirements/actions and only the requested plate/row detail. Module-owned read operations supply inventory/transfers; no cross-module raw writes. Every reference is checked under org/lab scope.

Extend owning search operations with stable cursors and explicit totals/truncation semantics. Add a selection-resolution operation that accepts typed entity/sample/set/container queries or explicit references, exclusions and a snapshot token, and returns exact material membership/duplicates/issues. Reuse sets when a reusable collection is saved. Apply to experiment subjects atomically under expected version. A filter that changes while selection is being resolved yields a new preview, not a silent changed experiment. Persist identity membership and selection origin, not frozen physical stock availability.

Extend the designer with a version-checked **complete/recalculate existing draft** operation using the same underlying compiler as `designer.start`. It preserves manual changes where valid, reports stale overrides/changed generated content, and returns persisted IDs/versions. One operation receipt distinguishes completion from a duplicate new design.


#### C1.W1. Implementable existing-record workspace contract

W1 starts from fetched main `a6e0d6233b621202d29a3b0e049a7ac4a6847a0d`, which includes the earlier experiment-to-transfer entry. The contract is `packages/schema/src/workspace.ts` and `operations/workspace.ts`. This subsection narrows the first delivery; it does not claim W2–W6 or A2/A4/A18 are complete. No new scientific record, persisted shared UI state, arithmetic formula or calculator is introduced.

`experiments.workspace` is a read with `{id, expectedVersion?, view}`. `id` is an experiment in the caller's org/lab; `expectedVersion` checks its current version, omitted only for initial entry. `view.panel` is exactly `design`, `plates` or `transfers`. Strict schemas reject unknown fields. Each panel has optional `page: {offset, limit}` and `detail: {id, version}`. Defaults are offset 0 and limit 20; maximum limit is 50. The response always includes named experiment and campaign summaries, question/stage/direct subject-entry count, validated `selection: {experiment, version, view}`, and a usable relative `href`. Workspace summaries never include raw record attributes or expanded subject membership. Readiness is the existing stored readiness, not a newly calculated scientific verdict.

- **Design:** `related` is a paged list of named direct experiment references: campaign, subjects, assay template, pinned protocol SOPs, their explicit experiment bindings, and documents. It does not recursively expand SOP defaults, sets or inventory. Each entry gives relation, record summary and whether that relationship pins its version. Experiment editors, calculations and confirmation use their existing operations and authority; opening Design does not fetch all 5,000 subjects into model context.
- **Plates:** `maps` pages current non-archived maps whose `attributes.experiment` is this experiment. Optional `map: {id, version, plate, wells?, relatedPage?}` selects one current map version and one 1-based plate. Selected wells are unique, at most 64, and must exist on that plate. `selectedMap` returns named map identity, exact generated plate count and only this plate's wells (at most 1,536), using the existing map calculation path. It also pages its direct layout, labware, control and selected-plate subject references; it never returns all generated plates or all map subjects. The selected map need not appear on the currently paged map list. `relatedPage` pages those references independently.
- **Transfers:** `plans` pages current non-archived plans whose `attributes.experiment` matches. Optional `plan: {id, version, group?, groupsPage?, rowsPage?, relatedPage?}` selects a current plan and optionally one persisted group ID. `selectedPlan.groups` pages group metadata and exact transfer counts, without movement arrays. `selectedGroup.rows` pages the selected group's persisted transfers in order, with original zero-based row index and the source/destination `PlanPlate` definitions for each returned row, including tubes/reservoirs. `rowsPage` without a group is invalid. `selectedPlan.related` pages direct plan labware/container references plus instrument/liquid/liquid-class/worklist references from the selected group, or all groups when none is selected. It does not recursively expand inventory, execute transfers, or calculate export eligibility. Existing transfer editors/checks/export remain the authoritative operations with their existing whole-plan confirmation scope.

Every page reports `items`, requested/defaulted `offset`/`limit`, exact `total` for that named collection, and `hasMore = offset + items.length < total`; an offset beyond the end returns an empty page and `hasMore: false`. List records sort by `(name, id)`, related entries deduplicate by `(relation, id, version)` and sort by `(relation, name, id, version)`, groups/rows retain persisted order. Pagination is a live read, not a frozen material-selection snapshot: independent creates/archives can change list membership between calls. The supplied experiment/map/plan versions still protect selected scientific context. W2 owns search and durable selection snapshots.

`detail` resolves only against the complete direct reference scope described above for the active panel and selected map/plan, independent of which related-reference page is visible. Without a selected map/plan, that panel's detail scope is only the experiment's direct references. The response returns one `WorkspaceDetail`: the related summary/readiness plus its existing kind-owned overview (at most 12 identity parts and 50 facts), never arbitrary attributes. Invoke the module overview builder on the exact validated record envelope, not `records.overview` by latest ID for a historical pin. Facts retain labels, values with units, detail, tone and source field; attach that field's evidence from the exact envelope where present, with absence remaining unknown. Secondary overview lookups (names, locations, live inventory) retain existing live semantics and are explicitly labeled `relatedRecords: current`; do not imply a historical stock snapshot. `omittedIdentityParts`/`omittedFacts` disclose any display bounds, with full-record navigation available. This gives useful stock, protocol and instrument facts inside the experiment using existing renderers. The chosen version must equal the relationship's explicit pin, or the current record version for a live/unpinned reference. If multiple relations identify the same exact record/version, use the first in the stable related-entry ordering. Selected map/plan records themselves already have their own detail projection. Full scientific editing may reuse existing fixed application editors and module operations in context; W1 must not promise a generic renderer or inventory stock calculation. The **Open full record** secondary action carries a return link to the workspace.

All references are re-read under the caller's org/lab. The producer validates experiment membership, selected record versions, map plate/well membership, group membership and the returned row endpoints before returning a descriptor. It must not accept `detail` merely because an arbitrary ID exists in this lab. The module's existing read adapters/operations own their scientific interpretation; workspace orchestration does not add raw cross-module writes.

### C2. Assay-driven fields and scientific targets

Extend essentials to reference a declared material role, and represent analyte/target selection explicitly on the appropriate method/template role. Bind the chosen kit through existing product/role references. If a new field is required, define its scientific type, unit/reference basis, valid target kinds and source of requiredness; do not put it in generic presentation metadata.

Supported field presentation metadata may group existing fields, supply lab labels/help and indicate editor type. It cannot carry formulas, arbitrary validation code, hidden defaults or user-authored executable HTML. Registered schema/domain rules own applicability, units, constraints and calculations. An agent can draft a new template using existing supported parameter types; an unsupported scientific type requires implementation before it can be used.

One scientific target has one owner: dose/dilution/cells-per-well in Design, independent dispense/source inputs in Transfers, calculated outcomes from calculator operations. All references retain basis (e.g. original specimen dilution or final assay concentration), units and evidence. Reference kit standards/control roles/readout requirements to the selected method, with no invented vendor facts.

**Experiment-specific choices must survive recalculation.** Extend the experiment with typed `designSettings.replicates` and `designSettings.layoutOverrides`; extend its existing keyed `controls` with typed material binding, destination role/region, count/scope and concentration or series where applicable. Control targets may reference a declared experiment-bound SOP variable instead of storing a second value. Treatment/assay volume already owned by `protocol[].inputs` stays there; a layout override references that input when it controls placement. Labels/notes remain explanations, never the canonical dose, series or replicate count. Final schema names may change in W0, but these ownership and precedence rules may not be left to the frontend.

Experiment layout settings own the design-wide placement policy and logical pattern; plate-map overrides own concrete per-plate/per-well exceptions. A change to the policy must preview affected exceptions and retain compatible ones, with conflicts requiring explicit resolution. Do not store an independently editable copy of the same strategy/seed in both owners. Seed ownership and propagation into generated maps must be explicit in the W0 schema examples.

For each supported value, resolve **explicit experiment choice → pinned template override/default → owning pinned SOP/layout default → missing**. The field's declared semantic binding identifies its owner; conflicting defaults across owners require resolution, not a last-write-wins choice. No hidden overwrite from the reusable template on recalculation. The compiler returns the resolved typed design with per-value origin, then both design totals and map generation consume it. Override precedence does not waive method constraints: incompatible targets or counts refuse with an actionable issue. Changing one experiment does not mutate the reusable template/layout; saving a new reusable template remains a separate operation. Recalculation preserves unrelated valid well edits, reports conflicting overrides for resolution and invalidates affected downstream generations/outputs. A fixture must change technical replicates and a positive-control dose, reload and recalculate, prove exact changed wells/totals/transfers, and prove the original template/layout unchanged.

### C3. Addition intent and generated plans

Persist a small typed addition definition on the experiment's design, keyed by stable ID and referencing the pinned protocol part/SOP step, material role, target map/well set, inputs and explicit dependencies. It is the scientific preparation intent. Generated `transfer_plan` records remain the authority for concrete movements. Each generated plan/group links back to the addition and exact experiment/map/input versions; accept neither unlinked rows nor a label as coverage evidence.

Model instrument-independent intents only where a deterministic planner exists: concentration dosing, specified-volume addition and supported dilution/seeding calculations. Extend existing SOP/domain calculators where they already own the quantities. Steps with unmodeled science can remain an explicit unresolved/manual preparation requirement; no fabricated worklist.

Track current well composition through **declared** additions and relevant removals/replacements. A wash is not an accumulating volume addition. Incubation/evaporation/loss must not be guessed. Scope a “target achieved” result to the specified endpoint and known composition. A plan covering Echo compound + vehicle transfers cannot claim that the required assay/base mixture has also been added. A vehicle well matches the intended dosing-well solvent content, not simply the maximum permitted solvent percentage.

For multiple sources or large plate sets, define deterministic assignment and partitioning with explicit plate/well/addition coverage. Shared source demand, dead volume and any validated line/priming/refill losses are aggregated before accepting the chosen preparation scope. Unknown stock remains unknown.

**Intermediate dilution contract:** implement generation-scoped planned intermediate identities and explicit producer/consumer references, reusing transfer-plan containers, preparation groups and existing dilution functions. One producer plan owns each intermediate well's complete preparation; all consuming plan/group versions reference its assigned draws and stock/diluent lineage. Calculate exact per-point demand, routes and the declared solvent-matching endpoint before partitioning the result into bounded plans/worklist files. A software size limit must not create another physical preparation or change its scientific endpoint. Prefer keeping related work together; support shared intermediates across partitions when required. This is an implementation direction following the focused Astra source assessment, not a claim of existing cross-plan support.

Sharing is limited to the selected generation and explicitly compatible preparation scope. Capacity or declared handling/stability/batch-independence constraints can require separate physical preparations, each with a distinct identity and its own residual volume/material cost. Do not infer reuse across experiments, generations or days. Calculate preparation count, plate count, stock/diluent demand and residual explicitly; do not silently introduce a policy trading stability or independence for material savings. A local-only scope reduction would require a product decision.

Require full validated stock/diluent preparation and method-required mixing before dependent use, with preparation volumes checked against the actual preparation device and labware capacity. All assigned draws must fit the available prepared quantity while retaining required residual. Original-source demand includes direct doses plus every preparation exactly once; dead volume is charged per physical well, not per software partition. Current readiness only establishes that an earlier group filled the well at all, so full supply/composition/dependency validation is required. Planned preparation never creates measured inventory. Unsupported dilution routes, preparation devices or required mixing remain explicit gaps; multi-stage serial dilution is not implied by the current single-intermediate optimizer.

**Candidate and accepted generations are distinct.** A generation identifies the exact addition/plate scope, input pins, all partition plan/group versions and the accepted generation it would replace. Calculation/regeneration creates a candidate only: it neither changes the accepted membership nor releases its reservations. Aggregate preview checks the proposed replacement after subtracting only the explicitly named predecessor scope once; unrelated plans' reservations remain. Rejection, interruption or a failed check leaves accepted membership and demand unchanged.

An authenticated person's version-bound **Confirm replacement** accepts the complete stated scope in one transaction using the existing operation/change-set authority. Recheck dependencies, coverage, all candidate partitions and expected accepted generation; then atomically switch accepted membership and reservations. Reviewing/confirming individual candidate partitions cannot activate their reservations or make a mixed old/new generation exportable. This requires a transfers-module contract change: existing reservations currently count active confirmed plans until execution/archive. Do not emulate the switch with independent frontend confirm/archive calls. Concurrent replacements of the same accepted scope conflict; partial compilation remains a resumable candidate and has no acceptance receipt. Independently accepted additions may remain separate, but every exported scope states its generation and joint source checks.

Superseded plans/files remain historical, without active reservations for the replaced scope. Routine Generate worklists operates on the currently accepted generation and rejects superseded candidates/plans; historical outputs remain downloadable with their exact version and historical label. Changing an experiment's accepted design marks related old preparation unsuitable for the new design, but does not silently release its demand; release/abandonment is an explicit authorized change. No generation transition creates an execution record.

Do not duplicate plan 018's workflow graph, schedule, duration engine or execution state. Addition links here express design dependencies and coverage. Future workflow creation consumes these accepted intentions and transfer plans.
